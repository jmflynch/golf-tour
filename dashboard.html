How it went

Strokes over par, hole by hole. Lower is better; the tick is the turn.

Pink Starburst went out in 41 and came home in 42, and from the 4th hole on, nobody got closer than two shots.

The 5th did it. 125 yards, and Pink Starburst made 2 — circled on the card, and the only red number anybody would write down until he birdied the last. Four shots clear on one swing. Percocet Tiger squeezed it back to two by the 7th and that was the last real threat of the day; Pink Starburst answered with par at the 8th and par at the 9th to turn in 41. Behind them Phil Dickelson had already lost touch, taking 7 at the 4th and 6 at the 117-yard 8th to turn in 47.

Phil Dickelson never got into it. He came home in 47 without a single hole under par — seven bogeys and two doubles — and drifted from six back at the turn to eleven at the end. Pink Starburst's one loose hole came at the 13th, 384 yards and the number two handicap, where he made 7 and let Percocet Tiger back to four. He closed par, birdie. Percocet Tiger went 6 and 6. Four became eight over the last two holes.

Two red numbers on the whole card and both of them belonged to Pink Starburst. Pink Starburst 83. Percocet Tiger 91. Phil Dickelson 94.

How it went

Strokes over par, hole by hole. Lower is better; the tick is the turn.

Percocet Tiger won this one on the first three holes and spent the next fifteen handing it back a piece at a time, never close enough to actually lose it.

Par, par, par to open, and that was the entire margin. Phil Dickelson dropped a shot on each of the first three and was three down before either of them had hit a genuinely bad shot. The 7th did the real damage — 400 yards, the number three handicap, and it took a 7 out of Phil Dickelson and a 6 out of Percocet Tiger. At the turn it was 42 to 47 and the front nine was over as a contest.

The back belonged to Phil Dickelson, for whatever that was worth. He birdied the 11th — the only red number on either card — and cut five back to three. Percocet Tiger made four pars all day and not one of them after the 6th, played the back nine in 47, and won the round anyway. The margin sat on three from the 11th to the last, wobbling to four for exactly one hole at the 15th. They both made 7 on the closing par 5, which was about right.

Percocet Tiger 89. Phil Dickelson 92.

How it went

Strokes over par, hole by hole. Lower is better; the tick is the turn.

Percocet Tiger had this round by the throat from the 6th hole to the 13th, then lost it in about twenty minutes.

Percocet Tiger turned in 39, Phil Dickelson 42, Pink Starburst 44 — and Pink Starburst's 44 was a 38 with two haymakers buried in it, a 7 on the 3rd and an 8 on the 481-yard 8th. That 8th is the number one handicap hole and it took a piece out of everybody; nobody made par.

Superstition is where it turned. Percocet Tiger gave a stroke back on the opening par 4 and never found it again, then made 5 on a 105-yard par 3 at the 14th and the lead was gone. Pink Starburst played the nine in +1 with birdies at the 11th and the 15th — the only two red numbers on the entire card — took sole possession at the 14th, and was never caught.
